How booking works

  • Blocks, not meetings: each SLP/OT holds one weekly block (1:30–4:00 shown = ten 15-min increments), bookable by any PC on any child on that professional's caseload — zone is irrelevant to booking.
  • Booking is the flag: the PC names the child and the question when booking, so the professional can prepare. Unbooked increments revert to the professional's documentation time.
  • Reverse booking: professionals can book a PC into their own block ("15 minutes about child T") — keeps professional-initiated flagging alive.
  • Supervisor visibility: supervisors see their PCs' bookings by default (awareness even without attending) and can opt to join any slot — complex or cross-discipline cases become a matched mini-clinical on demand.
  • Loop-back rule: consult outcomes that change a child's plan surface in the session note, the supervisor connect, or the next 1:1 — bookings are consult channels, not decision venues.

Open considerations (deliberately unresolved)

  • No default co-presence: all scopes on a family's team are never scheduled together. A monthly cross-scope case conference gives co-presence a floor; the pilot should measure how often it's actually pivotal rather than assume either way.
  • Under-booking blind spot: the model serves confident bookers best. New/novice PCs may not know what to book — consider a fuller default review cadence for them.
  • Capacity gauge: a chronically full block = demand signal for that professional; chronically empty = demand is being met elsewhere (or questions are being sat on — check which).
  • Group connect cadence: 1:00–1:30 window shown with existing anchors (provincial on Mon 1, zone-wide mid-month); whether the remaining Mondays carry an optional zone huddle or stay free is a dial, not a decision yet.
  • Supervisor connect length: shown as 30 min weekly per PC; could consolidate with the biweekly 1h individual supervision into a single weekly ~45-min supervision time.
How this differs from the current zone-clinical structure

Today (zone-led)

One clinical per zone per week. OT, SLP and supervisor attend for the full duration; each PC joins for a 30-min caseload slot. Cross-zone professionals attend two meetings (~4h/wk); the attending professional may not have worked with the child discussed (Central alternation); professionals sit through many cases outside their scope or caseload.

This model (professional-led)

Consultation follows the professional's caseload, not the zone: one bookable block per professional per week (~2.5h, demand-driven, always caseload-matched). Accountability stays zone-shaped: weekly supervisor↔PC connects with room for deeper data/fidelity review in BCBA scope. The collective layer lives in the 1:00–1:30 window (provincial / zone-wide / optional huddle). Cross-zone professionals: one block, zero passive sitting, no staggering constraints.

Coverage assignments and bookings are illustrative — adjust names, blocks and increments to actuals. Companion to “Clinical Meeting Design Space — Working Document” (this is Architecture 3: professional-led booking; Architecture 1: alternation; Architecture 2: biweekly all-scope).
